EU Digital Product Passport FAQ for Batteries

Page contents

    For general DPP questions, see the main FAQs

    1. Implementation timeline, guidance and readiness

    1.1 When will the detailed list of Battery Passport data points be published?

    A document detailing the Battery Passport data points has been published by the Commission. It is available on the Digital Product Passport (DPP) website under ‘Resources and Documentation’ and at this link. This identifies: data required from February 2027; data required only in particular circumstances; data not required in February 2027; and data applicable only to particular types of battery.  

    1.2 Will support be available while companies test the Registry?

    A helpdesk has been operational since the launch of the Registry on 20 July 2026. It operates from 08:00 to 20:00 Central European Time and assists with onboarding and use of the testing environment. The helpdesk is intended to address Registry functionality and onboarding, not policy interpretation. Policy questions will instead be addressed through technical guidance, webinars and other Commission materials.  

    You can now consult the Registry User Guide for economic operators and the Digital Product Passport website. To familiarise yourself with the Registry’s features and workflows, you can access the testing environment here. 

    1.3 How should the industry prepare if standards or technical guidance are published shortly before implementation?

    Companies can already start preparing based on the requirements laid down in Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (Batteries Regulation), as well as the guidance document detailing the Battery Passport data points published by the Commission.  Six out the eight DPP harmonised standards supporting the implementation process are cited in Official Journal. 

    Additional guidance documents will be made available to support timely preparation.  You can now consult the Registry User Guide for economic operators, the Battery Passport data points and the Digital Product Passport website. To familiarise yourself with the Registry’s features and workflows, you can access the testing environment here. 

    1.4 When will the remaining implementing acts be published?

    The Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1778 (DPP Registry Implementing Act) is published and accessible at this link. The implementing act defining access to restricted data is still being developed following stakeholder consultation and is expected in draft form for public feedback on ‘Have your say’ in either October or November 2026.  

    2. Responsibility for creating and maintaining the Battery Passport

    2.1 Can another company create or update the Battery Passport on behalf of the responsible economic operator?

    Yes. According to Article 77(4) of the Batteries Regulation, the responsible verified economic operator may give another operator written authorisation to act on its behalf to ensure the information is accurate, complete and up to date. This could include a third party, a non-EU manufacturer, a cell manufacturer or an original equipment manufacturer (OEM). Written authorisation does not transfer the ultimate legal responsibility. The economic operator placing the battery on the market remains responsible for compliance and for the data included.  

    It is important to distinguish the creation and updating of the Battery Passport from its registration in the DPP Registry. The creation and updating of the Passport concerns the Passport data itself, whereas registration concerns submitting the relevant information to the DPP Registry. The economic operator placing the battery on the market or putting it into service is required to register the battery passport in the DPP Registry in accordance with the provisions of the Batteries Regulation,Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) and the rules laid down in the DPP Registry implementing act. 

    2.2 Can a non-EU manufacturer register a Battery Passport?

    A non-EU operator responsible for the DPP can create an account and register a DPP in the Registry after completing verification under Article 4 of the DPP Registry implementing act, which applies to both EU and non-EU operators.  For Battery Passports, however, the registration has to be done by the economic operator that places the battery on the EU market. 

    2.3 Who is responsible when a finished battery is supplied by an EU battery manufacturer to an EU vehicle manufacturer?

    If the battery is already a complete, finished product when it is supplied, and the vehicle manufacturer merely installs it in the vehicle, the battery manufacturer is the responsible economic operator for the Battery Passport. The answer would be different where the vehicle manufacturer completes the battery, for example, by adding a Battery Management System (BMS) or other essential components, then the party responsible would be the vehicle manufacturer. 

    2.4 Does authorising a non-EU manufacturer to create or update the Battery Passport make them an EU manufacturer or importer?

    No, simply creating or updating a DPP does not make a non-EU entity the EU manufacturer or the importer. If an operator is authorised by written mandate to undertake practical Battery Passport tasks, the ultimate responsibility remains with the economic operator placing the battery on the EU market. 

    2.5 In cases where imported battery packs are integrated into larger systems in the EU, who assumes responsibility?

    The economic operator that completes the finished, safely functioning battery and places it on the market is responsible for the Battery Passport. Where imported packs and a BMS are assembled in the EU before the completed battery is placed on the market, the EU entity completing and placing that battery on the market (or putting it into service in case there is no placing on the market) would be responsible. 

    3. What constitutes the finished battery?

    3.1 At what level is the Battery Passport required: cell, module, pack or complete system?

    The definition of battery in Article 3(1) of the Batteries Regulation implies that the battery is a finished and (safely) working product that can deliver electrical energy. The Battery Passport applies to the finished battery. It may contain multiple cells, modules or packs. For a traction battery, the finished battery would normally include the BMS and any other components necessary for safe operation. 

    3.2 If several battery packs share a common BMS or other common systems, is one Battery Passport required for the complete system or one for each pack?

    If the packs depend on a common BMS or other common components and together form one working battery, the whole system is considered the battery and requires one Battery Passport. If the packs are complete, independently functioning batteries, each is a separate battery and must be considered separately. The assessment must be made case by case. 

    3.3 How does the 2 kWh threshold apply when a system contains several batteries that are individually below 2 kWh but collectively exceed 2 kWh?

    It depends on what constitutes the finished battery. If each unit is already a complete, independently functioning battery below 2 kWh, then the units are assessed individually; if the units form one integrated system with common components, the complete system is the battery, and the combined capacity is relevant. 

    3.4 Are separately imported cells or modules subject to the Battery Passport requirement?

    Normally, imported cells or modules that are merely components and will be assembled into a finished battery in the EU, are not themselves treated as the finished battery. The EU entity that completes the battery becomes the manufacturer. A limited exception applies to “do-it-yourself” kits where cells or modules are placed on the EU market ready for use by the end-user with commonly available tools. 

    3.5 How should batteries integrated into machinery, vehicles, homes or other composite products be treated?

    The Batteries Regulation and the Battery Passport apply also to batteries that are incorporated into other products. The Battery Passport obligation remains attached to the finished battery, rather than necessarily to the larger machine, vehicle or other products. For a product imported into the EU with a finished and (safely) working battery installed, the battery is normally placed on the EU market when the product is imported, and responsibility lies with the importer. 

    4. Placing on the market, imports and customs

    4.1 Must the Battery Passport registration identifier be entered in the electronic customs declaration?

    No. Although this is required for DPPs governed by the ESPR, it is not required for Battery Passports. The Battery Passport must still be registered in the DPP Registry, but its unique registration identifier shall not be entered into the electronic customs declaration. the electronic customs declaration. 

    4.2 What happens if dynamic data is not yet available when an imported battery is registered?

    The data field(s) concerning information and data resulting from the use of the battery may be empty when a new finished battery is imported and registered because the battery has not yet been used. This does not prevent the Battery Passport from being registered. 

    4.3 Can unfinished battery packs, modules or other components be imported without a Battery Passport for later assembly in the EU?

    Components that do not yet constitute a finished, safely functioning battery may be imported for further assembly. The Battery Passport obligation arises for the completed battery when it is placed on the market. If customs authorities have doubts about whether an imported product is an unfinished component or a finished battery, they should consult the relevant national market surveillance authority. 

    5. Registry architecture, hosting and system integration

    5.1 How can a company connect its own system to the Registry?

    The Registry will support an Application Programming Interface (API), but registrations may also be carried out manually. Companies may also export Battery Passports from their own systems and upload them in bulk. The testing environment would allow companies to determine which method best matches their IT capabilities. 

    5.2 Who creates the unique product or battery identifiers?

    Economic operators can create the identifiers locally using one of the options provided in the relevant standards, or they can use a commercial service. The identifiers are stored with the Battery Passport and registered in the Registry. 

    5.3 What is the purpose of the proof of registration?

    It allows an economic operator to demonstrate that it has registered a Battery Passport, where proof is requested by a public authority or business partner. No automated mechanism for requesting or exchanging this proof was foreseen at this time. 

    6. Registration, verification and access rights

    6.1 How will an economic operator become verified in the Registry?

    Economic operators will create an account and verify their identity using one of the permitted means, a qualified electronic seal supported by a qualified certificate for electronic seal, issued by a qualified trust service provider pursuant to Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 (eIDAS Regulation); or after submitting evidence of their identity and of their establishment by means of a qualified electronic attestation of attributes issued under Union law that enables the identification of the economic operator.  

    Natural persons or sole traders will create an account and verify their identity, via a qualified electronic signature supported by a qualified certificate for electronic signatures in accordance with the eIDAS Regulation; or by means of an electronic identification means that meets the requirements of the eIDAS Regulation with regard to the assurance levels ‘high’; or an electronic attestation of attributes issued under Union law that enables the identification of the economic operator. 

    6.2 How long will verified status remain valid?

    Verification will expire when the electronic identification method used expires or, at the latest, three years after the initial verification. The economic operator (or value chain actor for products other than batteries) must then repeat the verification process. 

    6.3 Who will have access to the Battery Passport?

    Part of the Battery Passport is public (point 1 of Annex XIII of the Batteries Regulation). Another part can only be accessed by notified bodies, market surveillance authorities and the Commission (point 3 of Annex XIII).  

    Access to the other parts of the Battery Passport (points 2 and 4 of Annex XIII) will depend on the forthcoming implementing act on the Battery Passport. This act will define access rights and specify which of those parts of the Passport may be accessed by different users, such as recyclers and repairers. 

    In particular, the implementing act is intended to clarify which users may be regarded as persons with a legitimate interest for the purpose of accessing restricted information. It should also specify to what extent they can download, share, publish and re-use the information. 

    This is pending the adoption of the final legally binding act.  

    6.4 Who authenticates repairers, recyclers and other actors seeking restricted data?

    The forthcoming implementing act will determine who may access restricted data and what they may do with it, but not the detailed authentication mechanism. The DPP IT system is foreseen to be implemented in decentralised way. As such the authentication mechanisms cannot readily be specified or operated centrally. National registers of recyclers, waste operators and similar actors may help confirm the status of the different categories of actors. 

    6.5 Does the DPP Registry itself give access to the full Battery Passport?

    No. The Battery Passport is hosted by the economic operator or an operator who has written authorisation to act on his behalf. That economic operator or the operator who has written authorisation to act on his behalf manages access to the Passport data. The DPP Registry primarily records registration and metadata and facilitates access for public authorities where legislation provides for it. 

    6.6 Must users have organisational email domains?

    No, as per the Batteries Regulation, an organisational email domain is not required. An organisational email address is needed as a contact detail or for receiving notifications, but it will not be used to decide who is allowed to access information. 

    7. Data requirements, applicability and language

    7.1 Are all Passport fields mandatory for every battery from February 2027?

    No. Some fields apply only to certain types of battery or only where technically relevant. Other fields are linked to legal obligations that will not yet apply in February 2027. A detailed Commission Guidance document has been published identifying the information that is mandatory, optional, applicable only in certain cases, or does not have to be filled/displayed as of February 2027.  

    7.2 Which requirements will not apply when Battery Passports are introduced in February 2027?

    Carbon footprint, recycled content and due diligence information will not yet be required. Those requirements have their own application dates. Battery Passports created from February 2027 onwards will contain this information only for batteries placed on the market after such obligations become applicable. 

    7.3 What supply chain information must be included for carbon footprint, recycled content and due diligence?

    Supply chain data may need to be collected to calculate or substantiate the results, but the full set of data does not have to be included directly in the Battery Passport. The Battery Passport would eventually contain: the carbon footprint declaration and label; the recycled content data; and the public due diligence report. However, this information will not need to be provided as of February 2027. Specifically: for the carbon footprint declaration and label, their format still must be specified in upcoming implementing acts; for the reported recycled content figures, these will apply in accordance with Article 8 of the Batteries Regulation and the relevant delegated act. Finally, the due diligence report is required from August 2027 (in line with Article 48(2) of the Batteries Regulation). Additional confidential supporting information may have to be made available to authorities separately when asked. 

    For more detailed information on the implementation of the data requirements for Battery Passports, please consult our Guidance document here, which will be continuously updated.  

    7.4 In which languages must Battery Passport information be provided?

    Many Passport parameters are language neutral. Where information is not language neutral, it must be provided in the language or languages which can be easily understood by end users, as determined by the Member State in which the battery is marketed, usually specified in national legislation concerning marketing and sale of products to consumers. 

    7.5 Who is responsible for language compliance?

    The economic operator responsible for placing the battery on the market is generally responsible for ensuring that the Battery Passport complies with the applicable requirements. 

    7.6 Should non-applicable fields be visible or hidden in the user interface?

    The semantic definitions and the active data models do not contain non-applicable fields at all. Once the data points become required or optional, the corresponding semantic model and the data model will be defined in a new version to enable fluent evolution. 

    7.7 What thresholds apply to detailed material composition reporting?

    The 1% threshold in DIN SPEC and the 0.1% threshold in the draft CEN technical specification are both considered as potentially reasonable, provided the resulting indication of detailed composition is sufficiently detailed and not merely a general indication of chemistry such as “LFP”. 

    7.8 Which batteries with a Battery Passport are exempt from carbon footprint, recycled content or due diligence information?

    Carbon footprint information does not apply to non-rechargeable batteries. Recycled content reporting does not apply to batteries that do not contain the relevant materials (cobalt, lead, lithium and nickel) and in any case not to batteries with external storage only. The responsible sourcing/due diligence reporting field does not need to be completed where the battery contains none of the relevant materials (cobalt, natural graphite, lithium, nickel). 

    8. Dynamic data and batteries without connectivity

    8.1 When do dynamic data requirements become mandatory?

    Dynamic data requirements are expected to apply when the Battery Passport becomes mandatory in February 2027. They were not included among the requirements expressly identified as applying later. 

    8.2 Who is responsible for supplying and updating dynamic data?

    The economic operator placing the battery on the EU market remains responsible. The practical work may be delegated through written authorisation to another operator that may be better placed to obtain or update the data. 

    8.3 How frequently must dynamic data be updated?

    At a minimum, dynamic data must be updated when a change in the battery’s status occurs or is being considered, for example, when deciding whether to remanufacture, repurpose or recycle it. More frequent updating may be necessary for certain dynamic data for purposes that require the state of health to be monitored, if technically feasible and proportionate. However, if, for example, the state of health does not change materially from week to week, weekly updates would not be necessary. 

    8.4 What if the battery cannot communicate data in real time?

    The minimum necessary remains to make the relevant information available when a change of status is considered. Real-time or very frequent communication is not necessarily required. 

    8.5 What dynamic information must be provided when a battery has no BMS?

    Information on the status of the battery and on values for performance and durability parameters have to be provided when the battery is placed on the market and subject to change in its status. State of health requirements apply only where the battery has a BMS. For use related data under Annex XIII, point 4(d) of the Batteries Regulation, the legal text does not provide the same exemption.  

    8.6 Can dynamic data fields be empty when a new battery is first placed on the market?

    Yes, where it concerns data other than the status of the battery and the values for performance and durability parameters. Data generated during the battery’s operational life will generally not yet exist when a new battery is first placed on the market, so the corresponding fields may initially be empty. 

    8.7 Can an offline e-bike battery or similar Battery Passport be updated only when it is brought in for servicing?

    For an offline e-bike batteries, updating dynamic information when it is brought in for servicing seems appropriate. There is no precisely prescribed update frequency; see question 8.3, “How frequently must dynamic data be updated?”. 

    9. Repurposing, remanufacturing, repair and end of life

    9.1 When does a battery require a new Battery Passport?

    A new Battery Passport is required when a battery is repurposed or remanufactured, or when it has become waste but is then prepared for reuse or repurposing. Normally, a new economic operator then assumes responsibility. 

    9.2 Does a simple repair require a new Battery Passport?

    No. A simple repair does not require a new Battery Passport. Remanufacturing is a more extensive intervention and is specifically defined in the Batteries Regulation. 

    9.3 What happens to the Battery Passport when the battery becomes waste?

    Responsibility passes to the producer, producer responsibility organisation or selected waste management operator. If the battery is subsequently recycled, the Battery Passport ceases to exist. 

    9.4 Does a battery placed on the EU market before February 2027 require a Battery Passport if it is remanufactured after that date?

    Yes. If the battery is remanufactured after the requirements become applicable, it will require a Battery Passport and must comply with the relevant provisions of the Batteries Regulation. Some exemptions may apply, in particular exemptions relating to carbon footprint, performance and durability. Where the underlying regulatory requirement does not apply, the corresponding Battery Passport information is not required. 

    9.5 How should lineage between an original Battery Passport and a Battery Passport created after repurposing or remanufacturing be maintained?

    As per Article 77(7) of the Batteries Regulation, the new Battery Passport must be linked to the previous Battery Passport. The new economic operator can then update any information that is no longer valid, including its identity and relevant performance or durability information. 

    10. Compliance, verification and enforcement

    10.1 What happens if an economic operator’s verified registration expires while its batteries are still in use?

    The operator would no longer be able to perform additional Registry actions, such as updating or transferring the Battery Passport, until it renews its verification. The Battery Passport’s compliance status and any consequences would be determined by market surveillance rules. The Registry could subsequently reflect that status. 

    10.2 Is “liability” for defective or incorrect information governed by the Batteries Regulation?

    Regulatory responsibility is different from legal liability. The Batteries Regulation addresses responsibility for compliance but does not use “liability” in this context. Liability for defective products is governed by relevant Union law. 

    10.3 How will misleading commercial Battery Passport solution providers be addressed?

    The Batteries Regulation does not define or regulate ‘solution providers’ as a distinct actor, unlike the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation, which provides for the role of DPP service providers. Therefore, under the Batteries Regulation, the decision to rely on a commercial solution providers rests entirely with the economic operator, and their relationship is governed solely by the commercial agreements between the two parties, rather than by a specific legal requirements under the Batteries Regulation.  

    11. Technical standards, interoperability and data models

    11.1 Will the Commission publish an official Battery Passport specific data model?

    Yes, the semantic repository/rulebook will translate the legal requirements into the technical structure used by the DPP Registry and provide a consolidated overview of the data required for each product. The Registry will use this structure to check semantic completeness. 

    11.2 How do Battery Passport requirements align with other systems?

    According to Article 78(a) of the Batteries Regulation, the Battery Passport is designed to be fully interoperable and work alongside other digital product passports. In practice, this means that the underlying technical architecture, data formats, terminology, and data-exchange processes are compatible with other digital product passports and systems.  

    11.3 Are blockchain or zero-knowledge-proof approaches required?

    No specific technology, including blockchains, is mandated. Economic operators or their delegated providers may choose the technology they consider appropriate. Blockchain is not prohibited, but it is not required. Zero-knowledge proofs were not specifically discussed. 

    12. Cross-sector and broader DPP ecosystem

    12.1 How will Battery Passports interact with vehicle DPPs, Digital Building Logbooks and other product passports such as those for steel or textiles?

    The Battery Passport is fully interoperable and linkable through its unique identifier. Other regulations and systems can use this identifier to reference the Battery Passport and reliably interpret the contained semantic data.
